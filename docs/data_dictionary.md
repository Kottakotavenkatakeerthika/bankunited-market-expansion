# Data Dictionary

Every column in `data/processed/` gets exactly one row here, in the same pull request that adds it. The checks read this file on every build: a processed column with no row, or with two, fails the build, and so does a value that doesn't fit its row's Type. A row for a column that isn't built yet only raises a warning, so a column can be documented before it's built.

## Conventions

- **Keys.** `cbsa_code` and `county_fips` are 5-character strings with leading zeros kept (`01073`, not `1073`). `year` is the year a measure describes, not the year it was released. `quarter_end` is the last day of the quarter. `rssd_id` is the FFIEC RSSD ID, stored as a string.
- **Geography.** Every year uses one OMB delineation vintage (`DELINEATION_VINTAGE` in `src/config.py`). Counties map to metros through `data/reference/county_cbsa_xwalk.csv`.
- **Column names.** `<source>_<measure>` in snake_case, e.g. `cbp_establishments_count`, `sod_deposits_usd`.
- **Type** is what the checks hold every value to: `int` (whole numbers; a column stored as decimals passes if every value is whole), `numeric` (any number), `string`, `string(N)` (text exactly N characters long, such as `cbsa_code`) or `date`. Missing values aren't checked.
- **Units go in the suffix**, and the automatic checks rely on them:
  - `_count`: a count, must be 0 or more
  - `_usd`: whole dollars (convert from thousands at ingest), must be 0 or more
  - `_usd_net`: whole dollars that can be negative, such as net income or charge-offs net of recoveries. No range check is applied
  - `_pct`: a percentage from 0 to 100
  - `_ratio`: a ratio published as a percent (1.25 means 1.25%), kept exactly as the source publishes it. It can be negative or above 100, as return on assets or loans-to-deposits can, so no range check is applied
  - `_share`: a fraction from 0 to 1
  - `_hhi`: a Herfindahl-Hirschman index of concentration, the sum of every competitor's squared percentage share: from near 0 (many small competitors) to 10,000 (one competitor holds everything). Must be within 0 to 10,000
  - `_net`: a signed amount that can be negative, such as net migration or natural change. No range check is applied, except that a change in a share (`_share_..._change_net`) must be within -1 to 1.
- **Missing means missing.** Leave a value empty when the source doesn't report it. Never fill with 0.
- **Released** is when the source published the value. The Charlotte back-test can only use values released before the decision date it simulates. `data/reference/release_dates.csv` dates the first release of every source, dataset and year the tables read, with one row per `source`, `dataset`, `year`, `quarter_end` (Call Reports only) and `release_type` (`initial`, `revised` or `final`; QCEW's preliminary annual averages count as `initial`). A year counts as available from its earliest `release_date`. The tables hold the latest revised values, so the back-test uses revised numbers on first-release timing.
  - FRED and LAUS get one `initial` row per year, the date that year's values first came out; their spring revisions get no rows.
  - PEP gets an `initial` row per year from the vintage that first published it (Vintage 2015 through 2024), plus `revised` rows for the three files the table reads: the 2010-2020 intercensal file (2015-2019 population), Vintage 2020 (2015-2019 flows) and Vintage 2025 (2020 on).
  - Call Reports are dated at `quarter_end` plus 35 days. Reports are due 30 days after the report date, 35 for institutions with more than one foreign office ([FDIC FIL-10-2026](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/consolidated-reports-condition-and-income-first-quarter)). Amended filings get no rows.
  - Where no first-release date turned up, the row carries the earliest date the data can be shown to have been public, and its note says it is an upper bound.
  - CRE columns have no rows of their own: they read the same CBP county files and QCEW files as the `cbp` and `bls` rows. Bank-level CRA (`cra_bank`) shares the dates of the aggregate CRA release.

## Tables and keys

| Table | One row per | Key | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| `msa_year` | metro and year | `cbsa_code`, `year` | Every metro in `data/reference/msa_universe.csv` (393), 2015–2024 |
| `bank_quarter` | bank and quarter | `rssd_id`, `quarter_end` | Every FDIC-insured bank with an RSSD ID, 2015 Q1 through the latest quarter FDIC has published |
| `bank_msa_year` | bank, metro and year | `rssd_id`, `cbsa_code`, `year` | Every bank with an office (SOD) or small business lending (CRA) in a metro, 2015–2024 |
| `msa_naics_year` | metro, industry sector and year | `cbsa_code`, `naics_code`, `year` | Every metro-sector with a CBP row, 2015–2023 (387 metros, 20 sectors) |
| `msa_year_history` | metro and year, before the panel | `cbsa_code`, `year` | Every metro in `data/reference/msa_universe.csv` (393, of which 382 have values), 2010–2014. BEA only, with the same column names as `msa_year` |

Key columns are never empty and never repeat; the checks fail the build otherwise.

How the tables join:

- **A bank in a metro, next to the metro's economy:** `bank_msa_year` to `msa_year` on `cbsa_code` and `year`.
- **BEA back to 2010:** stack `msa_year_history` on top of `msa_year`'s `bea_` columns. The metros and column names are the same, so the two make one 2010–2024 series.
- **A bank's metro footprint, next to its financials:** `bank_msa_year` to `bank_quarter` on `rssd_id`, with one quarter of `year`. The June 30 quarter matches the SOD survey date, and the December 31 quarter closes the calendar year the CRA loans cover.
- **Organizations:** affiliated banks share `call_top_holder_rssd_id` in `bank_quarter`, and `sod_top_holder_rssd_id` (deposits) and `cra_bank_top_holder_rssd_id` (small business lending) in `bank_msa_year`. Group by these to treat them as one competitor.

**Known limitation: Connecticut before 2022.** In 2022 Connecticut replaced its 8 counties with 9 planning regions as county-equivalents, with new FIPS codes (09001-09015 became 09110-09190). The crosswalk maps only the new planning-region codes, so any source that reports Connecticut under the old county codes is silently dropped in the county-to-metro join; no error is raised. Confirmed in CRA: the 2015-2023 files use the old codes and the 2024 file uses the new ones, so all 5 Connecticut metros (14860, 25540, 35300, 35980, 47930) are missing for 2015-2023 and present for 2024. Checked in data-v0.4's tables on 2026-10-23: the Connecticut metros have LAUS values every year, PEP from 2020, CBP for 2022-2023, SOD from 2023, and BEA and QCEW for 2024 only. BEA reports Connecticut by its old counties through 2023 and by planning region from 2024, so `msa_year_history` has no Connecticut values either. Through data-v0.4 the BEA columns held $0 for these metros in 2015-2023 instead of staying empty, because BEA's API answers 0 for the planning regions before 2024; the BEA ingest now leaves a 0 empty. Doesn't affect Charlotte (16740), the only back-test metro in `data/reference/msa_universe.csv`. Owner: Austin (crosswalk). Flagged 2026-10-01.

**Known limitation: deposits booked at a main office (SOD).** Banks report deposits at the office where they're booked, and large banks book deposits that aren't local, such as big commercial accounts and online accounts, at their main office. That inflates `sod_deposits_usd` and the main-office bank's `sod_deposit_share` in headquarters metros, and it moves deposits between metros when a bank relocates its main office. Charlotte is the extreme case: FDIC puts the metro's 2024 deposits at $457.3 billion, and in past years FDIC data have shown Bank of America alone holding roughly 73% to 80% of the metro's deposits. The as-reported columns stay unadjusted so they match FDIC's published figures; `sod_main_office_deposits_usd` (in both tables) isolates the booked-at-headquarters part so the modeling sub-pod can choose an adjustment. The concentration columns come both ways: as reported, and with the simplest adjustment, every main office and every home-banking office left out (the `_ex_main_office` columns; see the next note). Owner: Austin. Flagged 2026-10-04.

Some banks book large deposits at a full-service home-banking office (FDIC service type 13) that isn't their main office. In 2024 such offices held $503.7 billion, 3.1% of metro deposits (1.2% in 2015), mostly in a few metros: Philadelphia ($252.7 billion at Capital One's office in Wilmington, DE, 41% of the metro), San Antonio (USAA Federal Savings Bank and Citibank, 72%), Raleigh (First Citizens, 45%), Jacksonville (EverBank, 25%), Los Angeles (East West Bank, 3%) and Pittsburgh (PNC, 7%). On 2026-10-26 the modeling sub-pod (Sonia and Matt) chose to leave these offices out of the `_ex_main_office` concentration columns along with the main offices; `sod_home_banking_office_deposits_usd` (in both tables) keeps their deposits visible. Leaving out main offices alone would let a bank's bookkeeping move the measure: USAA's San Antonio deposits sat at its main office through 2023 and at a home-banking office in 2024, which would take San Antonio's HHI from 2,213 to 3,713. With both left out it's 1,018 in 2023 and 991 in 2024. Leaving them out changes the `_ex_main_office` HHI and top-3 share in 629 metro-years (59 to 66 metros a year, and the 3-year changes that follow from them). It lowers 2024's HHI from 3,047 to 991 in Philadelphia and from 3,526 to 1,226 in Raleigh, and raises Jacksonville's from 3,279 to 4,552. Charlotte has no such offices, and the as-reported columns don't change. Flagged 2026-10-23, decided 2026-10-26.

The Connecticut limitation above applies to SOD too; the SOD ingest prints how many Connecticut offices it leaves out each year.

**Known limitation: withheld all-industry totals (CBP).** In 2015-2017 the all-industry row of 5 county-years is flagged D or S, and Census stores its employment and payroll as 0 instead of publishing them. Only 2 of the 5 are in a metro: Kalawao County, HI (Kahului-Wailuku), in 2015 and 2016. Eureka County, NV (2016 and 2017) is in the Elko micropolitan area and King County, TX (2016) is in no CBSA, so neither reaches a table here. The `cbp_` columns in `msa_year` read these as real zeros and the flags are not read, so Kahului-Wailuku is slightly low in 2015 and 2016. This is known and not corrected here; the sector table `msa_naics_year` below leaves a withheld cell out instead.

**Note: BankUnited's own footprint.** The `bankunited_` columns come from `bank_msa_year`, so they see offices (SOD) and small business loans (CRA), not every way BankUnited enters a market. Its Atlanta corporate banking office, opened April 7, 2022, isn't in SOD through June 30, 2024, and its Atlanta CRA lending stays under a dozen loans a year, so neither source shows that entry. Its Dallas office shows up in SOD from June 30, 2022, reporting $0 in deposits that year. Charlotte, the back-test metro, has no BankUnited office in any year, so its `bankunited_sod_` columns are 0 every year, and BankUnited's CRA loans there are 0 except 1 in 2015, 1 in 2017 and 3 in 2020: the 0s mean BankUnited is absent, not present but quiet. Through 2024 BankUnited has offices in 9 or 10 metros a year: Miami, New York, Tampa, Orlando and five smaller Florida metros in every year, Punta Gorda through 2018 and Dallas from 2022. Entry dates for the back-test need BankUnited's own announcements as well. Its CRA lending jumps in 2020, the year of the Paycheck Protection Program: 2,263 loans in Miami, against 413 in 2019, and some lending in 120 metros, against 45. Owner: Austin. Flagged 2026-10-23.

## msa_naics_year: one row per metro per sector per year

County Business Patterns by industry sector, summed from counties to metros. `naics_code` is the 2-digit NAICS sector as text: `31` covers 31-33, `44` covers 44-45, `48` covers 48-49, and `99` is unclassified. A missing county-sector row means Census omitted it, not that the value is zero. Census starts omitting county-sector rows with fewer than 3 establishments in 2017, so small sectors step down between 2016 and 2017 without any real change. A metro's sectors therefore add up to slightly less than its all-industry total in `msa_year` (about 1% at most). The table has no row for a metro-sector that no county reports, and it covers 2015-2023 because CBP has no 2024 file. In 2015-2017 the employment and payroll that Census withheld (flag D or S, or an EMPFLAG letter) are left out of the sum, so a metro's employment and payroll for those years cover only the counties that published them and are an undercount when a county is withheld; `cbp_counties_reported_count` shows how many did. From 2018 the values are used as published, with the noise Census adds.

**Note: zeros that mean "not reported".** Since 2020, banks under $10 billion in assets can elect the community bank leverage ratio (FDIC's `CBLRIND` is 1; 1,717 banks in 2025 Q2). They report no risk-based capital, and FDIC shows 0 for it, so `call_total_risk_based_capital_usd_net`, `call_total_risk_based_capital_ratio` and `call_cet1_ratio` are left empty for them; through data-v0.4 the two ratios held those zeros. A bank can keep the framework for up to two quarters after it stops qualifying (FDIC FIL-35-2020), so a few of these bank-quarters are over $10 billion, with empty risk-based ratios: Merchants Bank of Indiana in 2021 Q3, Q4 and 2022 Q2, and Capitol Federal Savings Bank in 2023 Q1 and Q2. Banks under $1 billion in total assets don't report uninsured deposits, so `call_uninsured_deposits_usd` is empty for them; FDIC also shows 0 for some larger banks, so every 0 in that column is left empty. FFIEC 002 filers (17 insured U.S. branches of foreign banks, six of them in the $10–100 billion asset range in 2026 Q2) hold no capital of their own and file no income statement; FDIC shows 0 for their leverage, CET1 and total risk-based capital ratios, ROA, net interest margin, efficiency ratio and net charge-off ratio, so those are left empty, and through data-v0.4 they held those zeros. FDIC also shows a CET1 ratio of 0 for some banks whose total risk-based capital ratio it does compute, nearly all non-deposit trust companies, so that CET1 ratio is left empty too (2,083 bank-quarters in data-v0.4). No bank-quarter is left with a leverage, CET1 or total risk-based capital ratio of exactly 0. Branches of foreign banks aren't stand-alone banks; filter on `call_report_form` to leave them out of a peer set. Owner: Austin. Added 2026-10-23, extended 2026-10-26 and 2026-10-28.

**Note: loans over $1 million.** Call Report Schedule RC-C Part II reports C&I and nonfarm nonresidential (commercial real estate) loans with original amounts of $1 million or less; the `_over_1m_` columns are the totals minus that part, as balances at quarter end. A loan is sized by its original amount (for a line of credit, the commitment), so a $1.5 million loan paid down to $800,000 still counts as over $1 million. Part II covers no other loan types, so construction and multifamily loans have no size split. Three cases are left empty rather than 0: FFIEC 051 filers that skipped March or September (the form requires Part II only for June and December, and FDIC shows 0 for a skipped quarter; the few that reported anyway are kept), FFIEC 002 filers (no Part II at all), and, in the `_over_1m_` columns only, a bank-quarter whose small loans exceed the loans they're part of. For FFIEC 031 filers, Part II covers domestic offices, so their C&I over $1 million also includes any C&I to U.S. addressees booked in foreign offices. No public source has these by metro; a metro figure has to allocate each bank's amount. Owner: Austin. Added 2026-10-15.
